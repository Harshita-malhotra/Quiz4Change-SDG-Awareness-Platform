Sustainable Development Goal 14: Life Below Water

Overview

Sustainable Development Goal 14 aims to conserve and sustainably use the oceans, seas, and marine resources for sustainable development. Oceans cover more than 70% of the Earth's surface and play a crucial role in supporting life, regulating the climate, and providing food and livelihoods for billions of people.

Why It Matters

The oceans are facing severe challenges, including pollution, overfishing, and habitat destruction. Protecting marine ecosystems is essential not only for the health of the ocean but also for maintaining biodiversity, ensuring food security, and supporting economic livelihoods dependent on marine resources.

Goals and Targets

  • Prevent and significantly reduce marine pollution of all kinds, particularly from land-based activities, including marine debris and excess nutrients.
  • Manage and protect marine and coastal ecosystems to avoid significant adverse impacts, including by strengthening their resilience and taking measures to restore them.
  • Regulate harvesting and end overfishing, illegal, unreported, and unregulated fishing, and destructive fishing practices.
  • Conserve at least 10% of coastal and marine areas, consistent with national and international law and based on the best available scientific information.
  • Increase scientific knowledge, develop research capacity, and transfer marine technology to improve ocean health and ecosystem services.

Call to Action

Join the global effort to protect life below water. Reduce your plastic use, support sustainable fishing practices, and raise awareness about the importance of marine conservation. Whether you're by the sea or far from it, we all have a role in safeguarding our oceans and marine life for future generations.
